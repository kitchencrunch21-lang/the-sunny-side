Windows 11 Widgets: Build a Morning Dashboard That Actually Helps

By Ryan Carter7 min read
The best dashboard is the one you actually glance at.

Every morning starts with the same small questions. What is the weather doing? What is on the calendar? What did I promise to finish today? Most people answer by opening four different apps and a browser tab, which turns a thirty-second check into a five-minute scroll through things they never meant to look at. The Windows 11 Widgets board exists to collapse that routine into a single glance. Whether it actually does depends entirely on how you set it up.

Out of the box, the board is a mixed bag: genuinely useful widgets sitting on top of a noisy news feed. Ten minutes of customization turns it into something better, a quiet morning dashboard that shows what you need and hides what you do not. Here is how to build it, widget by widget.

What the Widgets board is, and how to open it

The Widgets board is a slide-in panel filled with small live tiles: weather, calendar, to-do lists, photos, stocks, sports scores, and more. Each widget shows live information without requiring you to open its full app. Think of it as the lock screen, expanded into a full panel that you control.

There are three ways to open it. Press Win + W from anywhere. Click the weather readout on the left side of the taskbar. Or swipe in from the left edge on a touchscreen. Press Esc or click outside the panel to dismiss it. Of the three, Win + W is the one worth memorizing, because it works no matter what you are doing.

If Win + W does nothing, check Settings > Personalization > Taskbar; some work PCs disable widgets by policy.

Adding, removing, and rearranging widgets

Open the board and look for your profile icon or the settings gear at the top, then choose the option to add widgets. A gallery appears showing every available widget. Click the plus sign on any widget to pin it to your board. That is the whole adding process.

Removing is the mirror image: click the three dots in the corner of any widget and choose "Unpin widget." To rearrange, drag widgets by their headers into whatever order you like, or use the move options in the same three-dot menu. To resize, open the three-dot menu and pick Small, Medium, or Large. Large suits anything you read, like a calendar agenda or a to-do list. Small is plenty for glanceable numbers like a stock price or a second city's temperature.

There is no penalty for experimenting here. Unpinning never deletes your data; it only hides the tile. A good approach is to add everything that looks interesting, live with the full board for a day, and then keep only the widgets you actually looked at. The survivors are your dashboard.

The widgets actually worth your space

Not every widget pulls its weight. These are the ones that earn a permanent spot on a morning dashboard:

  • Weather: current conditions plus the hourly and daily forecast. The single most-opened widget for most people, and the one sitting on the taskbar by default.
  • Calendar: today's agenda at a glance, pulled from your Outlook or Microsoft account calendar. If your day lives in a calendar, this widget alone justifies the whole board.
  • To Do: your Microsoft To Do list, with checkboxes you can tick right inside the widget. Pair it with the calendar and you get the complete "what is today" picture in two tiles.
  • Photos: cycles through your saved memories. Purely decorative, and completely worth it on grey mornings when the algorithm surfaces a good one.
  • Watchlist: the stocks you follow, with live prices. Small size is plenty; you are checking a number, not reading analysis.
  • Sports: live scores for your teams. Add it when the season starts, remove it when the season ends.
  • Traffic: commute conditions before you leave the house. Useless if you work from home, genuinely valuable if you do not.

Notice what is missing from the list: the news headlines. Those live in the feed below the widgets, which is a separate problem with its own separate fix.

Taming the news feed

Below your widgets sits an endless scroll of news stories, and for many people it is the reason the whole board feels noisy. The good news is that you are not stuck with it. Open the board settings and look for the feed controls. You can hide individual publishers you never read, mark topics as not interesting, and in recent Windows versions you can collapse the feed or turn it off so the widgets own the entire panel.

My suggestion is simple: turn the feed off for one week and see what happens. If you genuinely miss it, turning it back on takes ten seconds. Most people never do. A dashboard you glance at for ten seconds serves your morning better than a feed you scroll for ten minutes, especially before coffee.

The ten-second rule A widget earns its place if it answers a question in under ten seconds. Weather: what should I wear? Calendar: what is first? To Do: what is due? If a widget needs more than a glance to be useful, it belongs in an app, not on the board.

Taskbar settings for the widgets button

The little weather readout on the taskbar is the Widgets board's front door, and you control how it behaves. Go to Settings > Personalization > Taskbar and find the Widgets toggle, which shows or hides the button entirely. Some Windows versions also offer an option to open the board when you hover over the button instead of clicking it.

Hover-to-open is one of those features people either love or hate. Fans like that the board appears without a click. Everyone else finds it popping up every time the cursor drifts left on its way to the Start button. Try it for a day; if it keeps surprising you, switch back to click-only and forget it exists.

What widgets know about you

Worth knowing before you go all in: most widgets need you signed in with a Microsoft account, and the weather and traffic widgets need your location to be useful. If either of those gives you pause, you have options. Location access for widgets can be limited under Settings > Privacy & security > Location, and signing out of the widgets experience disables the personalized widgets along with the news feed.

For most people the trade is reasonable; the point is to choose deliberately. Spend two minutes in the privacy settings, decide what you are comfortable sharing, and enjoy the dashboard without background worry.

Widgets versus notifications: do not confuse the two

A common mistake is treating the Widgets board like a notification center. It is not. Notifications demand attention; widgets wait patiently to be checked. That distinction is the whole reason the board works as a morning routine. You open it when you want information, glance, and close it. Nothing buzzes, nothing badges, nothing pulls you in.

Build your 60-second morning dashboard

Here is the entire setup as a routine you can do right now. Open the board with Win + W. Pin Weather at large size, Calendar at medium, and To Do at medium. Unpin everything you did not deliberately choose. Turn off the news feed, or at least hide the publishers you never read. Drag the three keepers to the top of the board in the order you check them. Close the panel.

Tomorrow morning, before opening a single app, press Win + W. Ten seconds, three answers: the weather, your first meeting, your top task. That is the complete habit. It replaces the four-app scavenger hunt with one keystroke, and it sticks because the board is faster than every alternative, including reaching for your phone.

Revisit the setup once a season: swap sports widgets as seasons change, drop traffic if you go remote. Ten minutes of tuning twice a year keeps it feeling made for you.

Glanceable mornings

Once your information is this organized, give your windows the same treatment: Snap Layouts arrange your whole screen in two clicks, which pairs perfectly with a dashboard you can check in ten seconds. Small upgrades, stacked together, are how calm mornings get built.
